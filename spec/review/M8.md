### The goals, scope, limits and assumptions behind them

- **Scope: PCM's live demo.** Reason: one workspace on live data proves the connection with the least work. The L2 workspace only, for performance and capacity management only. The inventory comes from VCF Operations and starts at the VCF instance; an object's ID is its VCF Operations resource ID. Class of service, region, environment and time zone exist only in the synthetic demo. PCM shows what the VCF vCommunity Adapter publishes and says "No data" when a value is stale. PCM stores no metrics. It keeps each Agent E2 answer with its trace ID and rating for the 30 days that Agent E2 keeps the trace. PCM may compute for display and never writes to VCF Operations. Lab facts may go to the cloud model. Actions, stories, cases and the calendar are out of release 1.0.
- **Scope: Two PCM demos, synthetic and live.** PCM keeps its synthetic ACME demo unchanged and adds a separate live demo as a new entry in the demo drop-down; no page switches between the two. Reason: the lab has too little activity to show Agent E2 at its best, and the live demo proves PCM reads real data.
- **Constraint: No data on a cluster's environment.** In release 1.0, neither the VCF vCommunity Adapter nor Agent E2 knows whether a cluster is production or non-production, and no caller sends it. Reason: no data source holds it in the lab.
- **Scope: The VCF vCommunity Adapter in release 1.0.** Reason: the adapter computes the KPIs that PCM shows and Agent E2 explains, so both must agree with it. It may change anything except its identity in VCF Operations. A metric's key comes from the adapter; its name comes from Agent E2's catalog. All scoring logic comes from Agent E2, including the score bands and the closure levels. Usable capacity is Total minus HA minus Buffer. A metric whose name says "peak" is a 20-second peak; others are 5-minute averages. Where a catalog row's interval and its name disagree, the catalog's interval is the rule.
- **Goal: Release 1.0 success.** VMware colleagues and customers who see the PCM demo find it useful and want to copy the repositories privately, to customise and deploy in their own private cloud; Agent E2 explains a real lab KPI correctly in the live demo; on isvara.io every knowledge file is a page people can read and bookmark, Agent E2 answers with sources, and the exam exists; every contract between the repositories is checked automatically. Reason: the author demos PCM to these people, and their wish to copy it is the first sign of “Value without the author”; isvara.io's pages today are deep, hard to navigate and hard to place in the whole.
- **Constraint: The lab is reached only from the author's laptop.** The lab's VCF Operations and vCenter are behind a VPN, reached from the author's Windows laptop with PowerShell 7; a cloud session cannot reach them. Work stays read-only against them unless a milestone says otherwise, and every change to the lab waits for the author. Reason: it is the author's own lab and network.
- **Assumption: The lab is too quiet to show Agent E2 well.** Status: confirmed by the author. Effect: the synthetic demo stays the main demo; a load generator may come later.
- **Assumption: Only people on the author's VPN reach the lab VM.** PCM has no sign-in in release 1.0; the VPN and the lab firewall are its access control. Status: confirmed by the author ("private, behind my VPN"). Check: M8's lab session confirms that the lab VM answers on no address outside the VPN.
- **Constraint: Spending on Vercel AI Gateway.** A test run that calls a model has a budget of US$100, counted on the money it spends. The full benchmark round never runs: Agent E2 is checked by its answer set of about 15 questions and the free automatic checks. A run of the answer set starts only when the author says go. After go-live, isvara.io stays within US$50 a month on the gateway alone; the Vercel subscription of US$40 is apart. The author tracks spending on vercel.com and raises a budget when needed.
