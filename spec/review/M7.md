### The goals, scope, limits and assumptions behind them

- **Constraint: The live isvara.io changes only after the author's side-by-side review.** The rebuilt site runs first as a parallel site that is live but not advertised; the author compares the two sites side by side, and isvara.io is replaced only when the author is satisfied. The comparison starts when the parallel site holds new content (M4 onward); until then it shows the old content. Reason: isvara.io is visible to the world, and a site replaced before it is fully tested shows its gaps to every visitor.
- **Goal: Measures of authority.** isvara.io's position and impressions for about 20 searches that define the field, from Google Search Console, monthly; returning visitors and questions asked, from the site's counters and question log, monthly; contributions received and accepted; referral by Broadcom, through the author's marketing. Reason: these show “isvara.io as the authority” moving.
- **Scope: isvara.io's features in release 1.0.** The three learning paths as navigation; articles rewritten from knowledge files; infographics; Ask Agent E2; contributions; a quiz with a mastery map, the exam and the 2am game; calculators on Agent E2's catalog; a weekly summary of changes. Dropped: the podcast, the Operations Score, the cost calculator and SLA builder, the self-assessment and a global leaderboard. Reason: an administrator comes back weekly for tools, an expert to ask and learning; the dropped features serve none of these or hold knowledge of their own.
- **Goal: Value without the author.** A customer installs from the manual without contacting the author; Agent E2 answers the questions people ask the author after a demo; PCM shows each role what the author would have pointed at. Reason: this is the test of “Knowledge inside the customer's private cloud” and “isvara.io as the authority”; every requirement can pass and the product still fail if a customer needs the author.
- **Scope: The product and how customers take it.** The product is Agent E2, the VCF vCommunity Adapter and PCM, taken together with this workspace. Reason: each part alone shows only part of the knowledge. The VCF vCommunity Adapter alone is the simplest deployment: one pak file, no Linux, without Agent E2. Its documentation presents it as one part of the whole and points to the knowledge. In release 1.0, PCM runs on a Linux VM in the lab, as a service, where it already serves its pages (system design). The Linux container with VKS integration that the author named on 2026-11-26 waits until after release 1.0. isvara.io is a separate public site that customers use but never install or copy. Customers copy the product by mirroring each repository into a private one, because a fork of a public repository on GitHub is always public. The manual says so.
- **Scope: isvara.io rebuilt.** Same look and feel, new functionality; the old site goes. Reason: nothing brings people back to isvara.io today, and they cannot tell what has changed. Its pages are AI rewrites of Agent E2's knowledge files, with no AI-speak. A cited source opens the knowledge file, with a note that it is written for a model. The injection guard sits in isvara.io, before it calls Agent E2. The "load-and-ask" design retires. Downloads and the weekly refresh of the Word books continue, as downloads. It is built in the fork e1-rahabok/isvara, served as a parallel site, and switched over in one release once the author has compared it with isvara.io; each feature waits for the author's review of the before and after.
- **Principle: isvara.io is built for the desktop; the phone invites.** Every page is designed for a desktop screen. On a phone, isvara.io offers only what suits a small screen, and invites the visitor to try the full site on a desktop; no desktop feature is squeezed onto a phone. Reason: the work isvara.io serves, reading at depth, asking with a screenshot or a CSV file, running a calculator or the exam, happens at a desk, and a feature cramped onto a phone teaches less than an invitation to the real one.
